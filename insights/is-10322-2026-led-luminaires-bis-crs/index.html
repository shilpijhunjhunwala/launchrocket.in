Revised IS 10322 Standards for LED Luminaires: What Changes and When

Published 27 November 2026
Ritik Bindra · India Market Access
7 min read
Share

BIS has revised the safety standards for seven LED luminaire categories under the Compulsory Registration Scheme. The last date for the old editions, first set at 2 August 2026, now falls on 2 February 2027. Every licensee registered under an old edition needs fresh test reports for its lead models before then.

A lighting brand holds eleven CRS registrations: downlights, panels, flood lights, street lights and a range of emergency exit signs. Every registration number is valid and every carton carries the Standard Mark. On 3 February 2027, any of those registrations that has not been moved to the 2026 edition of its standard stops being operative.

Nothing on the BIS portal will look different that morning. The registrations will not vanish. They will simply no longer cover the goods.

What changed

IS 10322 (Part 1):2014, the general requirements for luminaires, has been revised and published as IS 10322 (Part 1):2026. The seven Part 5 sections that apply to LED luminaires under CRS move to 2026 editions as well.

The BIS CRS list of standards now shows both editions for each of these categories, side by side. That is what concurrent running means in practice: until the last date, a product may be registered and tested to either edition. After it, only the 2026 edition counts.

The two BIS documents

  • Reg./IS 10322 Series/Guidelines/01, 20 March 2026. Issued by the BIS Registration Department. Guidelines for implementation of the revised standards under the Compulsory Registration Order. Set the last date of concurrent running at 2 August 2026.
  • Reg./IS 10322 Series/Guidelines/02, 31 July 2026. Issued two days before the original date. Extends the last date of concurrent running for all seven categories to 2 February 2027, and states that all other terms of the March guidelines remain the same.
Last date of concurrent running: 2 February 2027

The extension added six months. It did not change what has to be submitted, and BIS has given no indication of a second extension. Some secondary sources still quote 2 August 2026; that date is superseded.

What existing licensees must do

Clause 4.A of the March guidelines sets out four requirements.

  • 1
    Implement the revised standard by the last date
    After 2 February 2027, no licence remains operative where compliance with the revised standard has not been ensured.
  • 2
    Submit complete test reports for lead models
    Issued by a BIS recognised laboratory, for every lead model in the scope of the licence that was previously tested to the old edition.
  • 3
    Give an undertaking for the series models
    Confirming that the requirements of the revised standard have been implemented in the other existing series models.
  • 4
    Expect action if the date is missed
    Cancellation of the licence, or deletion of models from its scope, may be initiated.

The phrase that matters is complete test reports. This is not a paper exercise where a lab reissues an old report against a new edition number. The lead model goes back to a lab and is tested to the 2026 standard in full.

New applicants and scope changes

New applications (clause 4.B). Applications where samples were already with a lab under the old edition may be processed under it. Applications recorded after the guidelines may use either edition, but the old edition is allowed only up to the last date of concurrent running, with a declaration that the revised standard will be implemented by then. After that date, no licence is granted under the old edition.

Change in scope (clause 4.C). Adding models under the old edition is allowed only up to the date the licensee switches over or the last date of concurrent running, whichever is earlier. A brand that has already moved a licence to the 2026 edition cannot then add new models to it on the old one.

For anyone starting a new registration today, the practical answer is simple: test to the 2026 edition. A report to the old edition buys a licence that has to be retested within weeks.

What is new in the standard

The annexure to the March guidelines, provided for general guidance, lists the main changes. The revision of Part 1 follows the current international luminaire safety standard, IEC 60598-1, on which IS 10322 (Part 1) is based.

IS 10322 (Part 1):2026, general requirements

  • IPX9 added to the ingress protection classifications
  • EMF safety requirements
  • Changes to marking
  • A clause for fast rotating parts, such as moving fan blades in fan-light combinations
  • The PELV system
  • Additional requirements for SELV outputs from controllable control gear
  • Limits for touch voltage and touch current
  • A normative annexure for battery and EDLC operated luminaires
  • Extended photobiological safety requirements

Where products are most likely to fail

Because Part 1 applies to every section, a luminaire whose own section barely changed can still fail on the general requirements. Based on what the 2026 edition adds, these are the areas to check before a sample goes to the lab.

  • Emergency luminaires and battery backed products. Lithium cells and EDLCs now have their own requirements, both in Part 1 and in Sec 8. Products designed around older nickel chemistries need the most attention.
  • Smart and dimmable lighting. The new SELV requirements for controllable control gear and the touch current limits fall on luminaires with dimming, colour tuning or wireless control.
  • Flood lights with glass covers. The new glass breaking test in Sec 5 is a mechanical test the old file never covered.
  • Fan-light combinations. The fast rotating parts clause brings moving blades into scope of the luminaire safety test.
  • High output and blue rich sources. The extended photobiological requirements are the place a previously compliant design can pick up a new marking or a new limit.
  • Labels and manuals. Part 1, Sec 2 and Sec 5 all change marking. Artwork is cheap to fix before a test and expensive after.
A note on drivers and lamps

These guidelines cover the IS 10322 luminaire categories. LED control gear and self-ballasted LED lamps are registered under their own standards and are not part of this transition. But a driver change made to pass the new luminaire tests can affect the driver's own registration, so check both before you commit to a redesign.

A sensible sequence

  1. List every R-number and the IS 10322 edition each registered model was tested to.
  2. Identify the lead model in each series. These are the models that need complete reports. Check that the series grouping still holds against the 2026 edition.
  3. Book a BIS recognised lab early. Every lighting licensee in the country is working to the same date, and lab capacity tightens as it approaches.
  4. Pre-check drivers, batteries, covers and marking against the new clauses before samples go in, so a failure does not cost a second test cycle.
  5. Submit the reports and the undertaking well before 2 February 2027, leaving time for BIS queries.
  6. Plan stock. Goods manufactured and sold against a licence that is no longer operative carry the Standard Mark without cover. Align production runs and import shipments with the date your licence moves across.

The wider point

Standard revisions under CRS now follow a consistent pattern: a revised edition, a fixed period of concurrent running, complete reports for lead models, and an undertaking for the rest of the series. Lighting is the latest category to go through it, and it will not be the last.

Brands that keep a clean register of R-numbers, lead models and test editions move through each revision as a planned exercise. Brands that do not find out at renewal.
