India Crossed USD 27 Billion in Ecommerce Exports. Every Market Still Has Its Own Rules.

Published 27 November 2026
Launch Rocket · Global Market Access
6 min read
Share

Indian sellers have now crossed USD 27 billion in cumulative ecommerce exports through Amazon Global Selling. The fastest growing categories are also the most regulated ones abroad. As more first-time exporters join, knowing what each destination market requires is what separates the brands that scale from the ones that get their listings blocked.

The numbers

On 25 November 2026, Amazon reported that exports by Indian sellers through its Global Selling programme had crossed USD 27 billion cumulatively since 2015, with more than 1 billion Made in India products sold to customers worldwide. Chetan Krishnaswamy shared the numbers on LinkedIn the same week.

The jump from USD 20 billion to USD 27 billion took about a year. Seven billion dollars of exports in twelve months is a lot of new listings, in a lot of new markets, from a lot of sellers doing it for the first time.

Growth sits in regulated categories

The ten year growth rates by category are the more useful part of the announcement.

The top three, health and personal care, grocery and beauty, are the categories where a destination regulator is most likely to ask questions. A cushion cover needs a fibre label. A face serum needs a safety assessment, a responsible person and an ingredient list in the right format. The faster a category grows, the more first-time exporters it pulls in, and the more listings go live before anyone has read the destination rules.

The base is widening

Exporters now come from more than 200 cities and towns. Moradabad's metalware, Panipat's textiles, Bhadohi's rugs and Erode's home textiles are all selling directly to overseas customers. Amazon cited Erode at over USD 121 million in sales between January 2025 and the third quarter of 2026, with Moradabad and Anand close behind.

Many of these sellers are manufacturers who have exported for years through buying agents. Selling direct is different. When a buyer imported for you, the buyer carried the compliance file. When you list on a marketplace yourself, the file is yours.

Key markets, different rules

The same product needs different paperwork in each market. These are the requirements that most often stop Indian listings, by market.

MarketWhat to prepare
United StatesCategory rules from the FDA (food, cosmetics facility registration and product listing) and the CPSC (children's products need third party testing and a Children's Product Certificate). Clear English labels, correct country of origin marking and import documents.
United KingdomUK specific marking where it applies (UKCA, or CE where still accepted), a UK Responsible Person and SCPN notification for cosmetics, and a UK address on the label for regulated goods.
European UnionSince 13 December 2024, the General Product Safety Regulation requires an economic operator established in the EU for most consumer products sold online. Marketplaces must remove listings without one. Cosmetics also need an EU Responsible Person and CPNP notification.
GermanyPackaging registration in LUCID before selling packaged goods, and WEEE registration for electrical products.
FranceEPR registration with a unique identifier, the Triman logo and sorting information, and French language labels and instructions.
Italy and SpainEU rules plus local language labels and instructions, and packaging EPR obligations.
CanadaEnglish and French labelling for most consumer goods, plus category rules from Health Canada.
UAEConformity schemes for regulated categories and Arabic labelling for some products.
The EU change most sellers missed

Under the GPSR, an Indian seller with no EU entity cannot simply list a product in Germany or France. Someone established in the EU has to take responsibility for it: a manufacturer, an importer, an authorised representative or, as a fallback, a fulfilment service provider. Their name and contact details go on the product or its packaging, and into the listing.

Three things we see from the ground

1. Product rules differ by market

Cosmetics, food, electronics, toys and home products each carry their own labelling, safety and registration needs across the US, UK and EU. A label that passes in one market is often wrong in the next. Most of the fixes are cheap if they are made before printing, and expensive once stock is in a fulfilment centre.

2. The HS code decides more than duty

HS classification and documentation decide duty, clearance and margin in the destination market. A wrong code costs quietly: a higher duty than necessary on every unit, a shipment held for reclassification, or a preference claimed under a trade agreement that later has to be paid back. The same code also drives India side benefits. RoDTEP, duty drawback and RoSCTL now apply to courier and postal ecommerce exports, including shipments through Dak Ghar Niryat Kendras from 15 January 2026.

3. Readiness is cheaper in sequence

Marketplace readiness, from account setup and listing content to labels and packaging, is cheaper to do once, in the right order, than to fix after a listing is blocked. Sellers who get the compliance file ready first launch more slowly by a few weeks and then do not stop.

The wider point

The demand for Made in India products is proven. As the exporter base widens, the brands that scale will be the ones that know what each market requires before they ship, not after.
